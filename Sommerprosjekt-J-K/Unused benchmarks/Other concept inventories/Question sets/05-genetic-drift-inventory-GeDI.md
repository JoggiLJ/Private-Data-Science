# Genetic Drift Inventory (GeDI)

**Discipline:** Biology (evolution — genetic drift)
**Format:** 22 agree/disagree statements, organized under **four scenarios**, each with one to three question stems. 15 statements target a key concept; 7 target a misconception.
**Source:** Price RM, Andrews TM, McElhinny TL, Mead LS, Abraham JK, Thanukos A, Perez KE. 2014. *CBE—Life Sciences Education* 13(1): 65-75. **Open access, CC-BY.**
**Status:** ⚠️ Partial transcription. The full 22 statements are tied to scenario vignettes that live in the article's supplemental instrument and do not survive automated text extraction cleanly. Below is what could be reliably extracted from the article body (the four key concepts, the six misconceptions, the published example item, and representative statements). **For the exact 22 items in order, pull the CC-BY instrument from the source and verify.**

Structure (from Figure 1 of the article): *"Students evaluate a series of statements that follow a question stem about a scenario presented in a vignette."* Each statement is answered **A. Agree / B. Disagree**.

## Example item (verbatim from the article, Figure 1)

> **16.** Chance survival of some individuals occurred in some generations, but not every generation. — A. Agree  B. Disagree

## The four key concepts assessed (correct scientific ideas)

1. Genetic drift is unpredictable because it has a random component.
2. Random sampling error can result in populations that no longer resemble the parent population / can result in changes in allele frequencies.
3. Genetic drift can result in nonadaptive evolution.
4. Genetic drift can overwhelm the effects of natural selection, mutation, and migration (so an allele can increase in frequency by drift even if it is not favored by selection).

## The six misconceptions used as targeted (incorrect) statements

1. Genetic drift is natural selection / adaptation / acclimation to the environment that may result from a need to survive.
2. Genetic drift is not evolution because it does not lead to directional change that increases fitness.
3. Natural selection acts but genetic drift does not (drift only occurs when natural selection is not occurring).
4. Genetic drift is random mutation.
5. Genetic drift is gene flow or migration.
6. Genetic drift only occurs in small populations (random sampling error does not occur in large populations).

*(Statements marked with an asterisk in the article are "disagree-keyed." Scoring: all items weighted equally; authors recommend comparing pre/post raw scores and treating scores near 50% as guessing.)*
